Safe to leave running overnight

A helper that reads web pages and installs packages will sooner or later meet instructions written by someone who isn’t you. Tiny Systems assumes that happens, and limits what a helper can do with them. This page lists what’s closed, and, just as plainly, what isn’t.

What a helper can’t do

Touch the computer it runs on
Each helper has its own container. With extra isolation, which is on unless you turn it off when adding a garden, it also runs in the gVisor sandbox with a kernel of its own. Containers run without root powers either way.
Reach the whole internet
A helper reaches only the sites on its garden’s list. Every other address, and every name lookup for one, is refused. The garden’s settings show what was turned away, with an Allow button beside each.
Take your logins
No GitHub token, SSH key or cloud login goes inside a helper. Finished work comes back when you pull it to your Mac.
Be reached from outside
Helpers have no open ports. The app talks to them through the garden’s own control channel, with your access.
Use your access without asking
Opening a port, starting another helper or turning on a feature arrives as a card that shows every detail. Only your Allow carries it out, with your access, not the helper’s.
Answer for you from your phone
The phone page is off until you switch it on, reachable only over Tailscale, and every request is signed with a key that never crosses the network.

What it still can do

A security page that lists only strengths invites the reader to find the rest. Here it is.

Use its Claude or ChatGPT sign-in
By default the sign-in is stored inside each helper, where a tricked helper could read it. For Claude you can keep it out: turn on the key gateway (the key lives in the site filter, which adds it on the way out) or Claude Console federation (no key is stored at all). Either way, a helper can still spend your plan’s usage.
Send things to sites you allowed
The list decides where a helper can connect, not what it sends. If a site that accepts uploads is on the list, a tricked helper could post to it. Keep the list short; the refusals log makes that easy.
Run any command inside its sandbox
Helpers work without asking before each command; the sandbox is what makes that safe. Their questions are cooperative: a helper asks because it’s told to. What’s enforced is the sandbox, the site list and the missing keys.
Work with less isolation, if you choose
Turn extra isolation off, and helpers share the computer’s kernel inside a restricted container. Allow the phone page on Wi-Fi, and someone who can intercept traffic on that network could take over the pairing.

Why not just run the agent on my laptop?

An agent on your laptop works as you: your files, your SSH keys, your signed-in browser, your whole network. Many never ask before acting. Tiny Systems puts each helper on a separate machine, a small VM on your Mac or a computer you own, with none of that inside.

Developers can read the engine’s threat model, with every boundary and the code behind it. Everything is open source, MIT licensed.
